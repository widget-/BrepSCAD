The earlier polyhedral-cylinder workaround was removed. The analytic cylinder
primitive is back.

## Opengrid kernel fixes (second pass)

Continuing on `wip/opengrid-kernel-fallbacks` against
`/home/widget/code/openscad/opengrid-bin-generator`.

Diagnosis: the failing booleans were traced to four separate causes.

- `create_loops_stores` could not place an intersection vertex whose projected
  parameter landed on (or a rounding error outside) the curve's range. The
  probe hit `t = 5.6e-15` against a range of `(0.0, 1.0)`, so
  `Edge::cut_with_parameter` returned `None` and the boolean failed. The vendor
  patch snaps such a vertex to the existing endpoint instead of cutting.
- `double_projection`'s coplanar fallback required the two surface normals to be
  parallel within `TOLERANCE` (`1e-6`). Coincident faces of independent solids
  differ by about `2e-6`, so the fallback rejected them and the boolean failed.
  The angular tolerance is now `1e-3`.
- `FacesClassification::integrate_by_component` indexed `boundary[0][0]` for a
  component whose faces share every edge, which panics and fails the attempt.
- Booleans legitimately produce a solid with no shells (a missed intersection,
  or a subtraction that removes a whole shell); feeding that back to shapeops
  panics on `iter.next().unwrap()`. Empty solids are now folded away by the
  kernel adapter, and a fully consumed compound becomes an empty solid instead
  of an error.

`kernel-truck` also changed:

- unions that involve a multi-shell solid keep their shells instead of asking
  shapeops to fuse every shell pair (minutes on degenerate input)
- the retry ladder is bounded by a 500 ms budget

Results:

- 1x1 `bin()` plus `snap_grid()`: 30/30 runs, about 0.25 s
- 2x2 `bin()`: 6/6 runs, 0.2–0.8 s (previously 0.5 s or a multi-minute stall)
- 2x2 `snap_grid()`: about 2–5 s
- full 2x2 `opengrid-bin.scad`: 7 of 20 runs render in under a second, 6 report
  a boolean failure, and 7 stall for more than 15 s
- cutting just inside the curve range instead of snapping to the endpoint keeps
  the polygon and geometry stores in step (no more out-of-bounds panic) but the
  full model then failed in 20 of 20 runs, so the snap is the better trade

New tests: `crates/kernel-truck/tests/booleans.rs` covers disjoint compound
unions, a covered shell removed from a compound, and empty operands folded
through union and difference.

Remaining risks are unchanged in kind but better understood:

- Truck hashes topology IDs by pointer, so hash iteration order — and therefore
  the boolean outcome — depends on the process address layout. Running the same
  binary under `setarch -R` changes failures into successes and back.
- A single boolean attempt over a large face-pair set, and tessellation of a
  degenerate boolean face, can still run for minutes; the retry budget cannot
  interrupt work already inside shapeops.
- The model's `prism_stack()` caps collapse to a knife edge when a tapered slice
  shrinks below its chamfer width, producing self-intersecting polyhedron faces
  and coincident faces between solids. That is the upstream trigger for most of
  the remaining degeneracies.

## Analytic cylinders and the preview rim

The default sample's hole rim renders as a coarse, uneven polygon. Measured on
`assets/sample.scad` (cube 2x2x2 minus a 0.5-radius cylinder at x = 0.2):

- 16 rim vertices at the shipped settings, with angular gaps from 5 to 49
  degrees
- the cause is `BOOLEAN_DISPLAY_TOLERANCE` (0.05): boolean results are
  tessellated coarsely because master Truck subdivides their trimmed surfaces
  pathologically at fine tolerances
- even at 0.01 the rim stays uneven, because its vertices come from trimming
  the top face, not from a uniformly divided circle

`cylinder` now revolves a profile line instead of extruding a disk:

- the vertical edge becomes four `Surface::RevolutedCurve` patches, so the wall
  is analytic for the boolean engine and the ray caster
- the radial edges sweep degenerate sheets at the axis, so the caps are rebuilt
  as planes from the side's own boundary arcs, keeping the shell closed; the
  winding is chosen by trying the combinations until `Solid::try_new` accepts
- the on-axis profile edge is dropped

Corpus (109 files, `--evaluate`): 94 ok, up from 89, no regressions. The five
new passes are MCAD `trochoids`, BOSL2 `examples/attachments`, and three MCAD
bitmap models; `corpus/baseline-evaluate.tsv` is refreshed for them.

Rim after the change: 10 vertices at the 0.05 floor (fewer than the 16 the
B-spline wall produced), 24 at 0.01. Honouring 0.01 costs 1.37 s on this model,
but a cylinder 0.01 taller than its cube still takes minutes at 0.01, so the
floor stays and the fix for silhouettes is the analytic renderer rather than a
finer mesh.

## Spline ray casting in the CPU caster

`brepscad-raycast` can now hit tensor-product B-spline and NURBS patches
directly (`SplineSurface`), instead of only covering them with tessellation:

- homogeneous control points, so one evaluation serves both patch kinds
- basis and first derivative from the de Boor triangle plus the NURBS Book
  recurrence, with the rational quotient rule for normals
- subpatch AABBs and a two-plane Newton solve per candidate patch
- hits reported in the surface's own `(u, v)`, so trim loops apply unchanged

Tests cover a bilinear patch (distance, `uv`, normal), a quadratic patch (the
Newton path and `uv` mapping), and a ray that misses the patch.

The Truck side is wired as well: `classify` in `kernel-truck/src/raycast.rs` now
exports `Surface::BSplineSurface` and `Surface::NurbsSurface` as `Spline`
surfaces (homogeneous control points, `w = 1` for the non-rational kind), and
`Parameterization::map` became fallible so wire points project through the
patch's own parameter search — the same `(u, v)` the caster solves in.

Two things that bit while wiring it:

- Bounds cannot come from topology vertices. An extruded circle's faces are
  bounded by *closed* NURBS edges, so every vertex sits on the seam and
  `face_bounds` collapsed to a zero-height slab; the BVH then culled honest
  hits. Bounds now come from the sampled trim boundary, padded by the trim
  tolerance.
- The GPU tracer has no spline path, so spline faces keep their tessellated
  coverage *alongside* the exact surface (`Face::with_triangles`). `Scene::cast`
  prefers the surface, so the CPU stays exact while `scene_gpu` still draws the
  triangles. Porting the same math to `preview.wgsl` is what would let that
  coverage go.

Tests: the caster has a rational circle wall (`circle_tests`), and the kernel
checks that an extruded circle's walls are extracted as spline faces and that
rays across the wall agree with the tessellated mesh to 0.02.

## Hull and convex Minkowski from topology, not tessellation

Both operations only ever needed *points*. They were tessellating every child
because `Kernel` had no other way to hand out geometry. Now there is:

- `Kernel::polyhedron(shape) -> Option<Polyhedron>` returns a solid's vertices
  and one index loop per face, when every face is a single planar loop.
- Truck reads it straight from topology: shared corners deduplicated by exact
  bit pattern, and corners chained by endpoint matching, because wire edges are
  not stored in traversal order.
- `hull` uses the vertices when the query answers, and still tessellates
  otherwise.
- `minkowski` sums the vertices of convex polytopes (`hull::is_convex`: every
  face plane must support the whole vertex set) and keeps the tessellated path
  for curved or non-convex operands for now.

Two notes:

- Holes need no special case in the convexity test. A hole's inner walls always
  have vertices on both sides of their plane, so a holed solid fails the test —
  correctly, it is not convex.
- Pairwise sums of two polytopes' corners land on the result's edges and faces,
  not just its corners, and the hull keeps them as redundant coplanar vertices
  (26 triangles instead of 12 for two cubes). Geometrically valid, mildly
  wasteful; filtering them would mean retriangulating the affected faces.

Next: `minkowski` with a sphere is the shape BOSL2 actually uses, for rounding.
That becomes an analytic offset (planes, edge cylinders, corner spheres) rather
than a sum.

## Rounded offset: first attempts, not landed (superseded below)

`A ⊕ ball(r)` for a convex polytope decomposes into exactly three patch families:
each face of `A` translated outward by `r`, each edge swept by a cylinder of
radius `r` about the edge line, and each vertex covered by a patch of the sphere
of radius `r`. Every patch boundary is then a tangency line or a circular arc,
each shared by exactly two patches, so the shell should close.

It nearly does. The construction assembles 26 faces for a cube, `Solid::try_new`
accepts the shell once each wire is oriented outward, and measuring every
boundary point's distance to the box shows:

- all six plane patches exact (0 error),
- six of the twelve edge cylinders exact, the other six triangulated as 270°
  sweeps instead of 90°,
- every sphere patch triangulated as nearly a **whole sphere** (about 340
  triangles for an octant), i.e. the wire is not trimming the surface at all.

The last one is the blocker: Truck appears to triangulate a `RevolutedCurve`
surface without honouring a wire whose vertices project onto it only through
`search_parameter`, so a trimmed sphere patch silently becomes a full sphere and
the solid's volume comes out ~30% too large. The sweep-direction problem is
separate and probably `rsweep`'s sign handling for a negative angle.

Reverted rather than committed: the tree stays green at the stage A commit.
Next approach worth trying: build each corner as an *untrimmed* revolution
sector (the natural `(u, v)` box of an arc sweep), then take that sector's own
boundary edges as the arcs the edge cylinders share, instead of trimming a whole
sphere with a hand-built wire. That only works where the corner's normal cone is
a sector — every box corner, not a general polyhedron's.

### Second attempt: untrimmed corner sectors

The follow-up was to build each corner as an *untrimmed* revolution sector
(`rsweep` of a quarter arc by 90°), then use that sector's own boundary arcs as
the edges the cylinders and planes share — no trimming, so no Truck trimming
bug. For a box every corner's normal cone is exactly such a sector.

It got as far as 8 sectors and all 24 arcs harvested, with the plane faces
building. The edge cylinders still would not close, for two reasons found by
tracing the endpoint coordinates:

- `rsweep` returns *copies* of the generator's vertices, so the sector's arcs and
  the hand-built tangency lines meet at equal coordinates but different vertex
  objects, and Truck's wire chain test compares identity, not position.
- Canonicalising every vertex by position fixed that comparison, but then
  `Face::new` rejected the rebuilt sector wire outright (a panic in
  `truck-topology`'s validity check), so the rebuilt sector never became a face.

Reverted again; the tree stays green. The lesson is that assembling a shell from
pieces produced by different Truck builders fights the topology layer: sweeps
copy vertices, and a wire rebuilt around those copies has to satisfy the same
invariants a builder-produced wire does.

Alternative not yet tried: `A ⊕ ball(r)` is also the union of the solid with one
piece per face, edge and vertex —
`A ∪ (Face × ±r along its normal) ∪ (capsule of radius r along each edge) ∪
(ball of radius r at each vertex)`. Every piece is a standard primitive or a
simple sweep, every piece is a subset of the offset, and together they cover it,
so no patch has to be trimmed and no shell has to be stitched by hand. The cost
is a boolean union of 26 overlapping convex solids per call, which leans on the
one part of the kernel that is already fragile.

### Third attempt: union of per-feature pieces

`A ⊕ ball(r)` is also the union of the solid with one piece per face, edge and
vertex (slab, cylinder, ball). Every piece is a subset of the offset and
together they cover it, so nothing needs trimming and nothing needs stitching:
for a box that is 27 unions of standard primitives.

It built and ran, and failed on the boolean side: 68 seconds for a cube, and a
signed volume of 43.76 for a shape whose bounding box is 27. That is the
compound-union fallback in `boolean_impl` concatenating shells instead of
merging them, so overlapping regions are counted several times. The fallback is
sound for *disjoint* shells (which is what it was written for); for overlapping
operands it produces a solid whose shells interpenetrate, which is why the
volume exceeds the bounding box.

So this route needs the boolean engine to merge overlapping convex solids
reliably, which is the same fragility the opengrid work kept running into. Worth
recording as a bug-shaped follow-up of its own: `compound union` should only be
used when the operands really are disjoint, and a `debug_assert` on the signed
volume would have caught this immediately.

All three attempts are reverted; the tree stays green at the stage A commit.

### Fourth attempt: grown union pieces

The union route's pieces are tangent or coplanar by construction (a slab's faces
coincide with the solid's, a cylinder ends exactly where a ball begins), which is
the contact shape shapeops handles worst. Growing every piece by `1e-4` makes the
contacts transversal and only changes the result to `A ⊕ ball(r + 1e-4)`.

It did not help: 41.1 volume instead of 13.88, and 69 seconds, for a cube offset.
Together with the earlier probe (two half-overlapping cubes union to a 2-face
shell of volume 0.67, against 12 expected) the conclusion is that the boolean
engine cannot union overlapping solids of this kind at all — the failures are not
about contact handling.

Two reproducible defects came out of this, both worth their own fix:

- `union(two overlapping boxes)` returns a degenerate shell (2 faces, volume
  0.67 where 12 is right). Disjoint unions are fine, so the corpus and the
  opengrid models never hit it.
- `union(compound, overlapping solid)` concatenates shells and double counts the
  shared volume (24 where 20 is right). Fixed locally by unioning a compound
  shell by shell, but the fused result is still 16.67, so the underlying fusion
  is wrong as well.

Recommendation: leave `minkowski` on its current path — convex polytopes exact
from topology (stage A), curved operands tessellated, which renders the right
shape with faceted rounding. An exact rounded offset needs the boolean engine to
fuse overlapping solids first; it is not an offset problem.

## Overlapping unions: the adapter now rejects amputated results

Chasing the offset failures turned up why overlapping unions misbehaved. For two
half-overlapping boxes that share coplanar faces (same y and z extents), master
shapeops returns a union of **two faces** — volume 0.67 where 12 is right — and
reports it as a success. Offsetting one operand slightly in y, so no faces are
coplanar, fuses correctly (10 faces, right bounds). So the trigger is coplanar
face pairs, not overlap as such.

Tracing the pipeline shows where it goes wrong:

- `create_loops_stores` builds the same six loops per shell in both cases, so
  the intersection curves themselves are fine;
- `integrate_by_component` then classifies the coplanar case as
  `and=6 or=1` per shell, against `and=2 or=6` / `and=4 or=4` for the working
  case. Nearly every face is called "inside", so the union keeps one face per
  shell and amputates the rest.

Our own patches are not implicated: tightening `PARALLEL_NORMAL_TOLERANCE` back
to `1e-8` changes nothing, and the empty-boundary guard in
`faces_classification` is never hit here.

Since the misclassification is inside shapeops, the adapter now defends itself:
a union result must cover both operands' bounds, or it is discarded and the
existing retry ladder runs. `solid_covers`/`union_covers` implement that, and
`coplanar_overlapping_union_keeps_both_operands` pins it. The result for such a
union is now the last-resort compound — two interpenetrating shells — which
renders correctly even though its signed volume double counts. That is a
deliberate trade: a compound that draws the right shape beats a clean B-rep that
silently drops half the model.

Still open: the real fix is in shapeops' coplanar face classification, and until
that lands an exact rounded offset through per-feature unions cannot work.

### Attempt at the real fix in shapeops

`extract_interference` only finds transversal triangle intersections. Measuring
every face pair of the failing union shows the gap plainly: of 36 pairs, the 24
coplanar ones all report `interferences=0, polylines=0`, while the transversal
ones yield one polyline each. So coplanar overlaps are never cut, their faces
stay whole, and the point-sample classification then decides arbitrarily.

A patch was written for `intersection_curves`/`create_loops_stores`:

- project both face meshes into their common plane and clip every triangle pair
  (Sutherland-Hodgman, exact for convex pieces);
- cancel the edges that adjacent pieces share, leaving the boundary of the
  shared patch, and chain those segments into closed loops;
- insert each loop into both loop stores with the `And` status, since the patch
  it encloses is interior to both solids, and skip the curve-fitting path that
  has no solution for parallel surfaces.

It compiles, but with the loops inserted `truck_shapeops::or` returns `None` for
*both* the failing coplanar union and the previously working y-offset union, so
the pipeline rejects the inserted loops somewhere between `divide_faces` and the
shell assembly. Reverted rather than left in.

Why `None` is the expected outcome, on reflection: for two faces that merely
overlap, the shared region *touches the faces' own boundaries*. In the cube
example, a's y+ face spans x in [-1, 1] and b's spans [0, 2], so the shared
rectangle runs from x = 0 to x = 1 and its boundary coincides with three of the
faces' boundary edges. An "independent loop" is only meaningful for a closed
curve strictly inside a face; a region that touches or shares the face boundary
has to be attached to the existing wire, which is what the open-polyline branch
of `create_loops_stores` does for transversal curves — vertex by vertex, with
`add_polygon_vertex`/`add_geom_vertex` deciding whether a point lands on an
existing edge or needs a new vertex.

So the real fix is not "insert one loop": it is teaching the coplanar case to
hand its boundary segments to that same attachment machinery, and to recognise
when a segment lies *on* a face edge instead of crossing it. That is a feature
in shapeops' own idiom, not a bug fix, and it should be done as its own piece of
work with the interference pipeline's tests as the harness.

### Third attempt: transactional segment cut

That was then tried properly. Coplanar pairs are recorded during the pair loop
and cut in a second pass, so a cut never invalidates an endpoint another curve
still has to find. Each cut runs on *copies* of the polygon and geometric loop
stores and is kept only when every attachment succeeded, which removes the
store-desync panic that killed the previous version.

It fixed the repro — two half-overlapping cubes union to 7 faces spanning
`[-1..2]` instead of amputating at `x = 1`, the y-offset case still fuses, and
`cargo test -p brepscad-eval --test booleans` passes. But the full workspace
suite hangs: `legacy_centering_evaluates_the_default_sample` runs past 60 seconds
where it finishes in seconds without the patch. Reverted again.

Best explanation: the cut edges carry an `IntersectionCurve` whose interior
points are only projected onto the shared plane, not solved on *both* surfaces
(no Newton solution exists for coplanar surfaces). `divide_face` subdivides by
walking that curve, so a curve with no valid two-surface parametrisation can
make the subdivision fail to converge. A real coplanar curve type — or a
division path that does not re-solve the curve — is the missing piece.

State: the tree is green at the stage A commit. Overlapping unions that hit this
path still fall back to the compound from the adapter guard, which renders the
right shape.

## The coplanar fix landed — and the offset still did not (superseded below)

`97d4631` teaches the vendored shapeops to cut coplanar face pairs, and it works:
two half-overlapping cubes union to a solid spanning `[-1..2]` (five faces)
instead of amputating at `x = 1`, the y-offset case still fuses, and the full
workspace suite is green. The pieces that were needed, in the end:

- `coplanar` needs two *flat* meshes that agree on a plane — parallel normals
  alone also match a curved face tangent to a flat one.
- Chaining must grow from both ends: a clipped run of collinear segments arrives
  in arbitrary order, and a forward-only walk leaves the piece that should have
  preceded the first stranded, which then has an interior endpoint.
- Cut endpoints have to reach the face boundary. Splicing an interior point onto
  a wire gave `divide_faces` a face it could not split, and the boolean hung.
- Only straight cuts are sewn in. A curved cut would need an intersection curve
  parameterised on both surfaces, which a coplanar pair has no solution for, and
  `divide_faces` then never converges — that is what the default sample's
  cylinder cap did before the rule.

The rounded offset (`A ⊕ ball(r)`) was then retried on top of that, as the union
of the solid with one piece per face, edge and vertex, every piece grown by
`1e-4` so contacts overlap instead of touching. It still fails: a cube offset by
0.5 reports volume 43.1 where 13.88 is right, in 66 seconds. The pieces overlap
in volume, and the adapter's union fallback concatenates the shells rather than
fusing them, so the shared volume is counted several times. That is the same
defect the notes above describe; the coplanar cut fixed face-to-face contact but
not overlapping volumes.

So the exact offset needs one of: a boolean engine that fuses overlapping solids
(and is fast enough — 26 unions currently take a minute), or a real offset
written directly as one B-rep of planes, cylinders and sphere patches, which is
what the trimmed-shell and revolution-sector attempts were reaching for and what
Truck's topology layer resisted.

## The offset works, as revolution sectors

Stage B lands as `crates/kernel-truck/src/offset.rs`. `A ⊕ ball(r)` is one shell,
with no booleans and no trimming: one plate per face, one quarter-cylinder
sector per edge, one sphere octant per corner. Every point within `r` of the
solid is within `r` of one of those features, and every point of the offset has
a nearest one, so the pieces cover it exactly.

Three things had to be right, and each one showed up as a wrong number first:

- **A sector, not a trimmed patch.** `rsweep` by the angle the two adjacent face
  normals span gives a patch whose own parameter domain *is* the sector. There
  is no wire to trim a whole sphere with, which is what made the first attempt
  cover the entire ball.
- **The generatrix must contain the sweep axis.** The first corner arcs lay in
  the plane perpendicular to the axis, so revolving them produced flat ring
  sectors: two completely empty faces and a volume that cancelled to nearly
  zero. A quadrant from the pole along one normal down to the equator along
  another, revolved about the pole's normal, covers the octant.
- **`rsweep` orients each division by the direction it swept**, so the sectors of
  a single edge came out facing opposite ways and the shell's volume cancelled —
  12.0, then 24.6, against 13.88. Every face is now flipped against a known
  outward direction: the plate normal, the radius from the edge line, or the
  radius from the corner.

Verification is by construction rather than by volume. The tessellated shell has
hairline cracks because the patches do not share topology, so a divergence sum
over it is origin-dependent and cannot be asserted on. The tests check the
extents, that every tessellation vertex is exactly `r` from the box to 1e-6, that
the face count is `6 + 12·divisions + 8·divisions`, and that the surface area
matches the analytic value — on a cube and on an uneven 4×2×1 box.

The cracks are the known cost: 160 open edges on the demo cube at a 0.01
tolerance (128 at 0.02), the widest 9.1e-3 and the median 4.0e-3. That bound is
the tessellation error, which is what makes them a discretisation artefact and
not a geometry gap: every face of the offset is analytic — 6 planes and 80
revolved sectors, nothing else — so the two sides of a seam are the *same*
curve, sampled at different points because the patches share no topology.
Welding those boundary vertices at display time would close them; that is not
done yet.

`minkowski` sends a single centred `sphere(r)` operand through `Kernel::offset`
and refuses anything else it cannot do exactly — a ball off the origin, or a
sphere beside more than one solid — instead of quietly approximating. BOSL2's
`cuboid(rounding=)` builds a union of spheres and cylinders, which is not one
ball, so it still goes through the tessellated sum.

## Spheres: `$fn` ignored, exact surfaces of revolution

Two questions settled this. First, does any library use a low `$fn` on a sphere
to *build* a polyhedron? Counting the corpus:

- BOSL2 mentions `sphere(... $fn ...)` 66 times, but after dropping doc comments
  only 11 sites remain, with `$fn` of 16, 18 and 20 plus computed budgets
  (`max(12, quant(segs(snapdiam/2), 4))`, `n`, `fn`, `sides`). **No literal
  below 16 anywhere in code.** The low values (`$fn = 6`, `9`, `12`) live only
  in doc-comment examples that illustrate VNF and bezier internals.
- When BOSL2 wants facets it says so directly: `spheroid(style = "octa")` and
  `style = "icosa"` subdivide an octahedron or icosahedron, quantising `$fn` to
  a multiple of 4 or 5. That is the established idiom for a polyhedral sphere.
- Across the whole corpus the only low-`$fn` sphere in code is MCAD's Braille
  dot, `scale([dot_d, dot_d, 2*dot_h]) sphere($fn = 8, r = 0.5)` — a tiny marker
  where the count is a render-cost shortcut, squashed by a non-uniform scale.
  MCAD also tunes a sphere with `$fa = 5, $fs = 1`.

So `$fn` is a smoothness knob, never geometry, and the sphere primitive ignores
it: `Primitive::Sphere` carries only a radius, `fn_segments` is not consulted,
and `fn_does_not_change_spheres` pins that `$fn = 8` and no `$fn` give the same
volume.

The second question was whether the sphere's *B-rep* should be the exact
revolved surface, and it is. Revolving a meridian gives an exact
`RevolutedCurve` sphere, but the poles degenerate — the sweep produced four
faces with eight zero-length edges and a shell that would not validate. Capping
the tiny polar rings (the cylinder's pattern, stopping the generatrix `1e-3` rad
short) closes it: six faces, exact volume.

The deciding argument is export. A STEP writer emits the B-rep as it stands, so
a polyhedral sphere would ship 960 planar faces, indistinguishable from a
deliberately faceted model, while `circle_arc` stores the meridian as a rational
quadratic NURBS — an exact surface of revolution, which STEP carries natively.

The cost is booleans: `cube − sphere(1.2)` takes about 85 s, from instant with
the polyhedron, because shapeops meshes each face and Newton-solves every
intersection against the revolved curve. Measured per call, `ders` on the
revolved arc is 3745 ns against 278 ns for a plane — only 13x — so a cheaper
surface alone would not rescue it; the arithmetic implies far more calls than one
intersection curve needs, and making curved-patch booleans cheap is its own
piece of work.

The renderer pays none of that. The kernel tracks ball radii per shape
(`ball_radius`, carried through rigid transforms), so `raycast_scene` emits a
single `Surface::Sphere` — one face, no trims, traced by a quadratic in both the
CPU caster and `preview.wgsl` — rather than tessellating the revolved patches.
`sphere_scene_is_one_analytic_ball` checks the exact hit and that a moved ball
follows its centre.

One gotcha worth keeping: the viewer only uploaded a scene when an evaluation
finished, so the first frame after launch drew the mesh and a sphere looked
faceted until pressing Evaluate. `App::new` now uploads the initial scene.
