## Analytic cylinder fix

The earlier polyhedral-cylinder workaround was removed. The analytic cylinder
primitive is back.

The actual regression was in tessellation of boolean-derived analytic results,
not in the boolean itself. The fix tracks solids produced by booleans and
propagates that marker through transforms. Only boolean-derived solids use a
coarser display tessellation tolerance (`0.05`); exact primitives keep the
requested tolerance. This preserves the analytic B-rep benefits and keeps the
release suite green.

## Opengrid kernel fixes (second pass)

Continuing on `wip/opengrid-kernel-fallbacks` against
`/home/widget/code/openscad/opengrid-bin-generator`.

Diagnosis: the failing booleans were traced to four separate causes.

- `create_loops_stores` could not place an intersection vertex whose projected
  parameter landed on (or a rounding error outside) the curve's range. The
  probe hit `t = 5.6e-15` against a range of `(0.0, 1.0)`, so
  `Edge::cut_with_parameter` returned `None` and the boolean failed. The vendor
  patch snaps such a vertex to the existing endpoint instead of cutting.
- `double_projection`'s coplanar fallback required the two surface normals to be
  parallel within `TOLERANCE` (`1e-6`). Coincident faces of independent solids
  differ by about `2e-6`, so the fallback rejected them and the boolean failed.
  The angular tolerance is now `1e-3`.
- `FacesClassification::integrate_by_component` indexed `boundary[0][0]` for a
  component whose faces share every edge, which panics and fails the attempt.
- Booleans legitimately produce a solid with no shells (a missed intersection,
  or a subtraction that removes a whole shell); feeding that back to shapeops
  panics on `iter.next().unwrap()`. Empty solids are now folded away by the
  kernel adapter, and a fully consumed compound becomes an empty solid instead
  of an error.

`kernel-truck` also changed:

- unions that involve a multi-shell solid keep their shells instead of asking
  shapeops to fuse every shell pair (minutes on degenerate input)
- the retry ladder is bounded by a 500 ms budget

Results:

- 1x1 `bin()` plus `snap_grid()`: 30/30 runs, about 0.25 s
- 2x2 `bin()`: 6/6 runs, 0.2–0.8 s (previously 0.5 s or a multi-minute stall)
- 2x2 `snap_grid()`: about 2–5 s
- full 2x2 `opengrid-bin.scad`: 7 of 20 runs render in under a second, 6 report
  a boolean failure, and 7 stall for more than 15 s
- cutting just inside the curve range instead of snapping to the endpoint keeps
  the polygon and geometry stores in step (no more out-of-bounds panic) but the
  full model then failed in 20 of 20 runs, so the snap is the better trade

New tests: `crates/kernel-truck/tests/booleans.rs` covers disjoint compound
unions, a covered shell removed from a compound, and empty operands folded
through union and difference.

Remaining risks are unchanged in kind but better understood:

- Truck hashes topology IDs by pointer, so hash iteration order — and therefore
  the boolean outcome — depends on the process address layout. Running the same
  binary under `setarch -R` changes failures into successes and back.
- A single boolean attempt over a large face-pair set, and tessellation of a
  degenerate boolean face, can still run for minutes; the retry budget cannot
  interrupt work already inside shapeops.
- The model's `prism_stack()` caps collapse to a knife edge when a tapered slice
  shrinks below its chamfer width, producing self-intersecting polyhedron faces
  and coincident faces between solids. That is the upstream trigger for most of
  the remaining degeneracies.

## Analytic cylinders and the preview rim

The default sample's hole rim renders as a coarse, uneven polygon. Measured on
`assets/sample.scad` (cube 2x2x2 minus a 0.5-radius cylinder at x = 0.2):

- 16 rim vertices at the shipped settings, with angular gaps from 5 to 49
  degrees
- the cause is `BOOLEAN_DISPLAY_TOLERANCE` (0.05): boolean results are
  tessellated coarsely because master Truck subdivides their trimmed surfaces
  pathologically at fine tolerances
- even at 0.01 the rim stays uneven, because its vertices come from trimming
  the top face, not from a uniformly divided circle

`cylinder` now revolves a profile line instead of extruding a disk:

- the vertical edge becomes four `Surface::RevolutedCurve` patches, so the wall
  is analytic for the boolean engine and the ray caster
- the radial edges sweep degenerate sheets at the axis, so the caps are rebuilt
  as planes from the side's own boundary arcs, keeping the shell closed; the
  winding is chosen by trying the combinations until `Solid::try_new` accepts
- the on-axis profile edge is dropped

Corpus (109 files, `--evaluate`): 94 ok, up from 89, no regressions. The five
new passes are MCAD `trochoids`, BOSL2 `examples/attachments`, and three MCAD
bitmap models; `corpus/baseline-evaluate.tsv` is refreshed for them.

Rim after the change: 10 vertices at the 0.05 floor (fewer than the 16 the
B-spline wall produced), 24 at 0.01. Honouring 0.01 costs 1.37 s on this model,
but a cylinder 0.01 taller than its cube still takes minutes at 0.01, so the
floor stays and the fix for silhouettes is the analytic renderer rather than a
finer mesh.

## Compute-shader analytic preview

The preview can now be ray traced on the GPU instead of rasterising triangles.
`BREPSCAD_ANALYTIC_PREVIEW=1` opts in; the mesh path stays the default and the
fallback.

- `EvaluationOptions::analytic_scene` makes the evaluator ask the kernel for a
  `brepscad_raycast::Scene` per top-level shape and merge them into
  `Evaluation::scene`.
- `Kernel::raycast_scene` is the new capability; Truck forwards to its inherent
  method.
- `crates/viewer/src/scene_gpu.rs` packs the scene into flat, `#[repr(C)]`
  buffers (nodes, faces, trim loops, trim points, vertices) with the tests
  pinning the layout, including that faces are emitted in BVH leaf order.
- `crates/viewer/src/preview.wgsl` mirrors the CPU caster: BVH traversal with a
  fixed stack, plane/cylinder/cone/triangle intersection, even-odd trim tests
  with periodic-`u` wrapping, and the raster path's shading.
- `crates/viewer/src/analytic.rs` owns the pipeline, camera uniform and
  dispatch; it writes into the same colour texture the raster path uses, so
  egui integration and resize handling are unchanged.

Verified headlessly: the viewer runs with the toggle on without validation
errors, and screenshots of both paths (`analytic.png`, `mesh.png`) show the same
cube-with-through-hole, same silhouette and lighting.

Not done yet: a UI checkbox (the env var is the toggle), GPU picking (the CPU
caster is ready for it), and spline surfaces still fall back to tessellation
inside the scene.

## Spline ray casting in the CPU caster

`brepscad-raycast` can now hit tensor-product B-spline and NURBS patches
directly (`SplineSurface`), instead of only covering them with tessellation:

- homogeneous control points, so one evaluation serves both patch kinds
- basis and first derivative from the de Boor triangle plus the NURBS Book
  recurrence, with the rational quotient rule for normals
- subpatch AABBs and a two-plane Newton solve per candidate patch
- hits reported in the surface's own `(u, v)`, so trim loops apply unchanged

Tests cover a bilinear patch (distance, `uv`, normal), a quadratic patch (the
Newton path and `uv` mapping), and a ray that misses the patch.

The Truck side is wired as well: `classify` in `kernel-truck/src/raycast.rs` now
exports `Surface::BSplineSurface` and `Surface::NurbsSurface` as `Spline`
surfaces (homogeneous control points, `w = 1` for the non-rational kind), and
`Parameterization::map` became fallible so wire points project through the
patch's own parameter search — the same `(u, v)` the caster solves in.

Two things that bit while wiring it:

- Bounds cannot come from topology vertices. An extruded circle's faces are
  bounded by *closed* NURBS edges, so every vertex sits on the seam and
  `face_bounds` collapsed to a zero-height slab; the BVH then culled honest
  hits. Bounds now come from the sampled trim boundary, padded by the trim
  tolerance.
- The GPU tracer has no spline path, so spline faces keep their tessellated
  coverage *alongside* the exact surface (`Face::with_triangles`). `Scene::cast`
  prefers the surface, so the CPU stays exact while `scene_gpu` still draws the
  triangles. Porting the same math to `preview.wgsl` is what would let that
  coverage go.

Tests: the caster has a rational circle wall (`circle_tests`), and the kernel
checks that an extruded circle's walls are extracted as spline faces and that
rays across the wall agree with the tessellated mesh to 0.02.

## Hull and convex Minkowski from topology, not tessellation

Both operations only ever needed *points*. They were tessellating every child
because `Kernel` had no other way to hand out geometry. Now there is:

- `Kernel::polyhedron(shape) -> Option<Polyhedron>` returns a solid's vertices
  and one index loop per face, when every face is a single planar loop.
- Truck reads it straight from topology: shared corners deduplicated by exact
  bit pattern, and corners chained by endpoint matching, because wire edges are
  not stored in traversal order.
- `hull` uses the vertices when the query answers, and still tessellates
  otherwise.
- `minkowski` sums the vertices of convex polytopes (`hull::is_convex`: every
  face plane must support the whole vertex set) and keeps the tessellated path
  for curved or non-convex operands for now.

Two notes:

- Holes need no special case in the convexity test. A hole's inner walls always
  have vertices on both sides of their plane, so a holed solid fails the test —
  correctly, it is not convex.
- Pairwise sums of two polytopes' corners land on the result's edges and faces,
  not just its corners, and the hull keeps them as redundant coplanar vertices
  (26 triangles instead of 12 for two cubes). Geometrically valid, mildly
  wasteful; filtering them would mean retriangulating the affected faces.

Next: `minkowski` with a sphere is the shape BOSL2 actually uses, for rounding.
That becomes an analytic offset (planes, edge cylinders, corner spheres) rather
than a sum.

## Rounded offset: attempted, not landed

`A ⊕ ball(r)` for a convex polytope decomposes into exactly three patch families:
each face of `A` translated outward by `r`, each edge swept by a cylinder of
radius `r` about the edge line, and each vertex covered by a patch of the sphere
of radius `r`. Every patch boundary is then a tangency line or a circular arc,
each shared by exactly two patches, so the shell should close.

It nearly does. The construction assembles 26 faces for a cube, `Solid::try_new`
accepts the shell once each wire is oriented outward, and measuring every
boundary point's distance to the box shows:

- all six plane patches exact (0 error),
- six of the twelve edge cylinders exact, the other six triangulated as 270°
  sweeps instead of 90°,
- every sphere patch triangulated as nearly a **whole sphere** (about 340
  triangles for an octant), i.e. the wire is not trimming the surface at all.

The last one is the blocker: Truck appears to triangulate a `RevolutedCurve`
surface without honouring a wire whose vertices project onto it only through
`search_parameter`, so a trimmed sphere patch silently becomes a full sphere and
the solid's volume comes out ~30% too large. The sweep-direction problem is
separate and probably `rsweep`'s sign handling for a negative angle.

Reverted rather than committed: the tree stays green at the stage A commit.
Next approach worth trying: build each corner as an *untrimmed* revolution
sector (the natural `(u, v)` box of an arc sweep), then take that sector's own
boundary edges as the arcs the edge cylinders share, instead of trimming a whole
sphere with a hand-built wire. That only works where the corner's normal cone is
a sector — every box corner, not a general polyhedron's.

### Second attempt: untrimmed corner sectors

The follow-up was to build each corner as an *untrimmed* revolution sector
(`rsweep` of a quarter arc by 90°), then use that sector's own boundary arcs as
the edges the cylinders and planes share — no trimming, so no Truck trimming
bug. For a box every corner's normal cone is exactly such a sector.

It got as far as 8 sectors and all 24 arcs harvested, with the plane faces
building. The edge cylinders still would not close, for two reasons found by
tracing the endpoint coordinates:

- `rsweep` returns *copies* of the generator's vertices, so the sector's arcs and
  the hand-built tangency lines meet at equal coordinates but different vertex
  objects, and Truck's wire chain test compares identity, not position.
- Canonicalising every vertex by position fixed that comparison, but then
  `Face::new` rejected the rebuilt sector wire outright (a panic in
  `truck-topology`'s validity check), so the rebuilt sector never became a face.

Reverted again; the tree stays green. The lesson is that assembling a shell from
pieces produced by different Truck builders fights the topology layer: sweeps
copy vertices, and a wire rebuilt around those copies has to satisfy the same
invariants a builder-produced wire does.

Alternative not yet tried: `A ⊕ ball(r)` is also the union of the solid with one
piece per face, edge and vertex —
`A ∪ (Face × ±r along its normal) ∪ (capsule of radius r along each edge) ∪
(ball of radius r at each vertex)`. Every piece is a standard primitive or a
simple sweep, every piece is a subset of the offset, and together they cover it,
so no patch has to be trimmed and no shell has to be stitched by hand. The cost
is a boolean union of 26 overlapping convex solids per call, which leans on the
one part of the kernel that is already fragile.

### Third attempt: union of per-feature pieces

`A ⊕ ball(r)` is also the union of the solid with one piece per face, edge and
vertex (slab, cylinder, ball). Every piece is a subset of the offset and
together they cover it, so nothing needs trimming and nothing needs stitching:
for a box that is 27 unions of standard primitives.

It built and ran, and failed on the boolean side: 68 seconds for a cube, and a
signed volume of 43.76 for a shape whose bounding box is 27. That is the
compound-union fallback in `boolean_impl` concatenating shells instead of
merging them, so overlapping regions are counted several times. The fallback is
sound for *disjoint* shells (which is what it was written for); for overlapping
operands it produces a solid whose shells interpenetrate, which is why the
volume exceeds the bounding box.

So this route needs the boolean engine to merge overlapping convex solids
reliably, which is the same fragility the opengrid work kept running into. Worth
recording as a bug-shaped follow-up of its own: `compound union` should only be
used when the operands really are disjoint, and a `debug_assert` on the signed
volume would have caught this immediately.

All three attempts are reverted; the tree stays green at the stage A commit.
