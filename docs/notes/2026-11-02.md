# Session notes — 2026-11-02

Snapshot after making booleans against the exact sphere cheap, and after the
analytic preview learned to trace splines. Current state lives in
[`../status.md`](../status.md).

## The cost was a parameter-search seed

Profiling `cube − sphere(1.2)` in release put about 90% of the time in
`truck_geotrait::algo::surface::presearch`, reached from
`IntersectionCurve::subs` → `double_projection` →
`Surface::search_nearest_parameter`. The `Surface::RevolutedCurve` arm sampled a
101×101 grid (10,201 surface evaluations) just to seed a Newton solve, even
though `RevolutedCurve` already implements an analytic search: project the point
into the meridian plane, solve the generatrix one-dimensionally, then refine in
3D. Every evaluation of every intersection curve paid that grid.

`vendor/truck-modeling/src/geometry.rs` now delegates to
`rotted.search_nearest_parameter(point, hint, trials)`. Measured on the release
CLI, `cube − sphere(1.2)` went from 8.99 s to 36 ms (about 250×); the debug test
suite does both sphere booleans in under half a second.

## The seam had to be canonicalized first

Delegating alone was fast but wrong: the sphere patches were trimmed to half
their area, and the lens volume came out as 4.40 instead of 6.38. Comparing the
old and new search call by call showed every difference was a seam alias — the
old grid returned `v = 0`, the analytic search returned `v = 2π` for the same
point (identical to 1e-16 in space). `Revolution::proj_angle` decides the seam
side from a cross-product sign that is rounding noise exactly on the seam, and
with no continuity hint `seam_side` had no reason to pick a side. The old grid
tie-broke to its first sample, `v = 0`.

`vendor/truck-geometry/src/decorators/revolved_curve.rs` now maps a near-`2π`
angle to `0` when there is no hint, *before* the Newton refinement. That is the
piece that matters: normalizing the returned parameter afterwards did not
reproduce the old result.

With both patches the boolean matches the old geometry:

| `cube` and `sphere(1.2)` | difference | intersection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| old grid search (release) | 1.8366 | 6.1639 |
| analytic + seam rule | 1.8361 | 6.1639 |

The two volumes sum to 8, and the lens is within 3.4% of the analytic 6.3804 at
the 0.05 boolean display tolerance.

## Regression test

`cube_sphere_booleans_partition_the_cube` in
`crates/kernel-truck/tests/booleans.rs` pins the carved and lens volumes, their
sum (the cube's volume), and a generous 10 s ceiling for both booleans. The pair
took about 18 s in a release build before this change.

## The preview traces splines too

The exact sphere patches still rendered faceted because the GPU preview only
had planes, cylinders, cones and balls. A `Surface::RevolutedCurve` whose
generatrix is a smooth curve was exported to no analytic surface, and native
B-spline/NURBS faces kept their tessellated coverage.

Two changes remove that fallback:

- `SplineSurface::from_revolution` builds the exact rational NURBS surface swept
  by a generatrix: `u` is the angular direction (scaled to `[0, TAU]`, so the
  existing periodic trim convention applies) and `v` keeps the generatrix's
  parameter. The kernel adapter transforms the control points by the face's
  `Processor` matrix and gives Truck the same surface for trim projection. The
  parameter order is the reverse of `RevolutedCurve`'s `(curve, angle)`, so the
  converted faces flip their normal relative to the B-rep face.
- `preview.wgsl` now packs knot vectors, homogeneous control points and
  subpatch bounds, and ports the CPU Newton solver (`basis_window`,
  `spline_frame`, `trace_spline`). The GPU no longer needs triangle coverage for
  any patch whose degree fits its fixed nine-point basis window.

Two numerical details showed up only in the rendered image:

- The subpatch culling boxes were padded by 5% of the patch span. Curved patches
  bulge farther than that, and rays were culled, leaving scattered holes. They
  are now padded by 25%.
- The f32 Newton solve kept the f64 residual threshold of `1e-6`; a fraction of
  rays never met it and showed as background speckles. It now accepts `1e-4`
  after 32 steps, which is well below a pixel at preview scale.

A third bug was found with the rounded offset (`minkowski` of a cube and a
sphere). `from_revolution` built the surface around the revolution axis but
never added the axis origin back, so every revolved face away from `(0, 0, 0)`
was displaced by its origin. The offset's corner octants therefore failed to
project their trim loops and fell back to triangles, which is why the rounded
cube still looked faceted. With the origin restored the offset scene is six
planes, 48 cylinder sectors and 32 spline octants, with no triangle fallback.
`rounded_offset_scene_is_fully_analytic` pins that, and a non-zero-origin sphere
test covers the conversion directly.

Verified with headless screenshots of a scene containing the sphere lens, an
extruded circle and a torus, and with CPU ray-grid tests for the extruded circle
and torus. The default cube-with-hole sample is unchanged.

## Still open

- The mesh fallback and the CLI's tessellation still use
  `BOOLEAN_DISPLAY_TOLERANCE`; the analytic preview is exact, but the raster
  path stays visibly faceted on boolean sphere patches.
- The rounded offset's patches still do not share topology, so a hairline seam
  remains where they meet. It is a display crack, not a facet: the analytic
  surfaces themselves are exact.
- A patch whose B-spline degree exceeds the shader's basis window still keeps
  tessellated coverage. Truck's usual degrees are 1–3, so this is a guard rather
  than a current limit.
- Truck booleans remain dependent on `Arc`-pointer hash order. Repeated runs of
  the same binary and `setarch -R` were stable for this case, so this change is
  not relying on a lucky layout.
