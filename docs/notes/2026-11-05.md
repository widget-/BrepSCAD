# Session notes — 2026-11-05

Follow-up on the OpenGrid snap cutouts. The 1x1 and 2x1 bin bodies are now
correct after the boundary-wire fix; the remaining artifacts are in the snap
clips.

## Symptom and reduction

The side clip cutout showed an upward-sloping right side and a messy wedge where
the horizontal and vertical cutouts meet. Isolated the two cutters from
`modules/snap.scad`:

- `shell`: `snap_outer_shell` alone.
- `h`: horizontal clip cutout (`prism_stack` base `[12, 0.4]`, 1.3 mm straight,
  0.2 mm at -45°).
- `v`: vertical back cutout (`prism_stack` base `[12, 0.6]`, 5.8 mm straight,
  0.3 mm at -45°).

Outer shell, `h` alone and `v` alone all match OpenSCAD exactly. The combined
subtraction is the failure:

| model | BrepSCAD | OpenSCAD |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `shell - h` | 3889.431265 | 3889.431265 |
| `shell - v` | 3854.706153 | 3854.706153 |
| `shell - h - v` | **3889.431265** (v dropped) | **3849.092103** |
| `shell - (h ∪ v)` | **3840.536694** (open, 92 welded boundary edges) | **3849.092103** |

The two cutters overlap by 0.4 mm in Y and 0.3 mm in Z. Their 45° end tapers
shrink the cross-section to (almost) zero, so the overlap is between two knife
edges. Moving them apart makes the result exact: `h_depth = 1.0` (0.1 mm gap)
or `v_height = 5.4` both render identically to OpenSCAD. A plain rectangular
cutout (no end taper) also gives the correct volume: `3848.376` vs OpenSCAD
`3848.361`.

## Engine failure

`BREPSCAD_DEBUG_BOOLEAN` shows the second difference returning
`EmptyOutputShell` at every tolerance, so the adapter falls back to the
unmodified subject. Stage instrumentation showed:

- `coplanar=false`, `planar_pair=true` for the `99 x 34` face pair.
- The `and` shell assembly fails: an intersection curve has two leader points
  only `~3e-10` apart. `altshell_to_shell` dedups them to one point and returns
  `None`, which aborts the whole boolean.
- The degenerate curve is at the crossing of the two knife-edge tapers. The
  raw intersection curves themselves are short but valid; the degenerate
  fragment appears after vertex insertion/cutting in the loops store.

## Attempted engine fixes (all reverted)

1. Skip intersection curves that collapse to a point in `loops_store.rs`.
   The degenerate curve was created later by vertex cutting, so the filter
   never saw it.
2. Snap near-endpoint vertices at the cut site (parameter-space tolerance
   `1e-5`/`1e-6` and a 3D `1e-6` endpoint check). This changed the result from
   "v dropped" to `3842.7`/`3828.9`, but the mesh stayed open (72–76 welded
   boundary edges) and the union path still produced a wrong shell.
3. Increase the parameter endpoint tolerance to `1e-4`: too aggressive; it
   skipped valid cuts and broke the cutter union (`4181.95`, 15 triangles).
4. Keep the raw endpoints of the degenerate curve: assembly succeeded, but the
   wrong face statuses produced wrong/open geometry.
5. Small tool shifts (`0.001`–`0.01` in X/Y/Z): no correct result; Z shifts had
   no effect, X/Y shifts changed the wrong result.

The experiment patches are in the session scratch:
`snap-engine-experiment.patch`, `snap-engine-experiment-2.patch`,
`snap-engine-experiment-3d.patch`. The working tree was restored to the
committed engine before the next decision.

## Planar-arrangement fix

The classic loop assembly cannot represent a near-tangent intersection that
collapses to a point. A planar fallback now handles those pairs:

- `coplanar/planar.rs`: for every planar face, compute the other solid's plane
  cross-section, split the face boundary at the segment endpoints, run
  `coplanar::divide`, and classify each piece with a point-in-solid test.
  `classic::and`/`or` call it when `process_one_pair_of_shells` returns
  `EmptyOutputShell`.
- A difference is `and(subject, neg(tool))`; the negated shell has negative
  signed volume, so the point-inside result is inverted for it.
- The fallback is gated to cutout-sized operands. `small_union` uses a bounds
  volume of at most 1000 mm³; `And` also requires the tool to be at most 10% of
  the subject's bounds volume. Large model booleans keep the normal pipeline.
- The kernel checks watertightness only for small unions. If a cutout union is
  open, it falls back to a two-shell compound; the evaluator subtracts cutters
  sequentially when the union fails. This keeps the union semantics for normal
  booleans while recovering the knife-edge cutouts.

## Practical model-level fix

The 45° taper ends are hidden behind the clip. Replacing each cutout's taper
with a flat end (a rectangular slot) makes the committed engine produce the
correct volume with no engine changes. A 0.05 mm flat tip also gets close but
leaves more open edges; the plain rectangular end is the cleanest result.
